When a user first opens HOU2ED, they are met with a sleek black screen that feels premium and modern, carrying only a glowing gold logo in the center with the “2” shining slightly brighter than the rest. The atmosphere is immediate: serious, trustworthy, and bold. Beneath the logo sits a short tagline in white, promising safety, stability, and second chances. At the bottom of the screen a gold spinner quietly turns, signaling loading without distracting from the central message. After a moment, the screen slides forward on its own, introducing the onboarding journey.
The onboarding is simple but emotional. The first screen appears against the same black canvas, this time with a bold gold heading that reads “Togetherness.” An illustration shows a small house icon, drawn in gold, with two figures standing side by side in white. Beneath the image, in calm white text, is a short body paragraph reminding the user that housing is more than shelter — it is connection. Progress dots sit at the bottom, white for the slides to come, one glowing gold for the current step. A “Skip” option rests discreetly in the top right in white, while a gold “Next” button anchors the lower right corner. The screen slides again to “Second Chances,” this time with the image of a key turning in a door, and once more to “No One Stands Alone,” illustrated with puzzle pieces locking together to form the outline of a home. Each screen is equally simple: black, gold, white, no clutter, nothing unnecessary. When the user reaches the final slide, the “Next” button transforms into a bold gold button reading “Get Started.”
Pressing this button fades into the role selection screen. The background remains deep black, but now two oversized cards dominate the middle. The first is a luminous gold rectangle, centered, with bold black text reading “Find Housing” and an accompanying home icon. The second is a sleek black rectangle with a gold outline, its text and icon in matching gold, labeled “List Housing.” A subtle line of white text beneath explains that providers can also search if they wish. Above these cards, a gold heading asks warmly: “What would you like to do today?” And at the bottom is a single gold “Continue” button, which only activates once the user makes a choice.
Once a role is chosen, the user lands on the authentication screen. The top row holds a small gold version of the HOU2ED logo on the left and a help icon on the right. Below it is a collapsible gold-titled panel that reads “What HOU2ED Means.” Expanding it slides down an elegant block of white text that tells the story: the 2 is glowing because it represents togetherness and second chances. Closing the panel tucks it away again. Two tabs sit side by side, one reading “Log In,” the other “Sign Up,” gold underline showing which is active. In the login tab, fields appear with thin white outlines against the black background. Users can enter their email or username, then their password, each field glowing softly gold when tapped. Beneath sits a bold gold button that says “Log In,” black text centered within. A small “Forgot Password?” link appears in white, opening a reset flow when tapped. Switching to Sign Up shows additional fields: full name, chosen username, email, password, and confirm password. At the bottom, a single gold button says “Sign Up.”
When the form is submitted, a modal slides up, presenting six empty input boxes for a six-digit code. The title reads in gold: “Enter the code we sent to your email.” As the user types, the cursor jumps to the next box automatically, and the numbers glow gold against the black. If they type wrong, a small red error appears just beneath the box: “Invalid code. Please try again.” A timer counts down from thirty seconds until “Resend code” in white activates. Successful verification flows them immediately into the app, and from then on they only log in with username or email and password.
For seekers, the first thing they see after logging in is the home screen, which doubles as the search experience. At the very top of the page sits a tall black search box outlined in gold, with a faint white placeholder that reads “Where do you want to live?” On the left of the box is a small gold location pin icon, while the right shows a glowing gold filter icon. Beneath the box lies a row of pill-shaped chips that provide one-touch shortcuts like “Immediate,” “Free,” “Veterans,” “Families,” and “Near me.” Inactive chips appear black with white borders, but the moment one is tapped, it fills with gold and its label turns black, signaling it is active.
The background is dominated by a map. The map itself uses a dark theme, with streets faintly traced in muted gray, and the pins glowing in gold. Each gold pin means a listing has beds available today; gray pins mean full. At wider zoom levels, a soft gradient of gold shifting to amber and red overlays the map, showing where housing is densest. The user can pan and zoom with natural gestures, and as the map shifts, the listings automatically refresh. At the bottom third of the screen, a deck of cards rests, swipeable left and right. Each card is framed with a gold border, showing a cover photo of the property, a title in bold gold, the neighborhood name in white, cost in white, and small rounded badges indicating availability (“Beds today” in green, “Verified” with a gold check, or special gold badges like “5150 capable” for hospitals). Swiping up on a card expands it fully; tapping anywhere on the card opens the full listing detail.
Navigating the app is consistent and minimal. A bottom navigation bar spans across the screen, a flat black background holding five icons: Home, Search, Messages, Saved, and Profile. Each icon is a simple outline in white that glows gold when active. This bottom nav is persistent throughout the seeker experience, providing a constant anchor.
When the user opens filters, the screen slides upward, covering the map. At the very top is a bold black bar with gold title text that says “Filters.” On the left corner is a white “Clear all” link, on the right a glowing gold “Apply.” The body of the page is long and scrollable, organized into neat sections. Each category, like Housing Type, Amenities, Accessibility, Cost and Payment, or Rules, appears as a collapsible accordion with its header in gold on black. Expanding a section reveals options as white-outlined checkboxes or toggles that fill gold when selected. Only numeric inputs like cost ranges or distance sliders require typing. The entire experience feels click-only, visual, fast. At the bottom, even as the user scrolls, the “Apply” button remains sticky, a bold gold strip across the footer. Tapping Apply slides the filters away and refreshes the map and list immediately.
Opening a listing feels polished. The screen is framed in black, with a photo carousel at the top. Each photo fills the width, with small gold dots at the bottom to show position, and subtle chevrons on either side to slide. Beneath the photos, the title appears in bold gold, followed by the provider’s name in white, with a gold “Verified” badge if applicable. A horizontal stat row displays “Beds available” with a green label, cost, intake method, and a “Last updated” timestamp, each marked with small gold icons. Sections below unfold as collapsible black cards, each with gold headers like “Overview,” “Amenities,” “Services,” “Rules and eligibility,” “Location map,” and “Reviews.” Expanding one smoothly reveals the content inside, all in white text. At the bottom of the screen, a sticky bar is split in two: on the left, a white outlined icon lets the user save the listing, while on the right, a bold gold button says “Apply Now.”
The application flow is straightforward and reassuring. The first step automatically prefills the seeker’s name and contact information from their profile. The next screen presents eligibility fields as simple toggles or tags, letting them indicate whether they meet key requirements. The document step provides upload fields styled as outlined black boxes with white text and a gold glow when tapped. Users can attach an ID, insurance card, proof of income, or referral letters, with errors flagged in red if formats are wrong. The final step shows a full review with all data entered, and a bold gold “Submit Application” button at the bottom. A confirmation screen then appears with a clean progress tracker showing “Submitted” in green and future statuses grayed out. From then on, the Applications tab lists each submission in a vertical stack of black cards, each labeled with its current stage, and each card linking to its dedicated message thread.
Messages feel familiar and clean. The inbox view shows black cards with the sender’s name in gold and the most recent snippet in white. Entering a conversation reveals chat bubbles: seeker messages aligned right in gold-bordered black bubbles, provider responses on the left in plain black bubbles with white text. Timestamps are shown in faint white beneath each bubble. A paperclip icon allows attachments, while a “Report” option sits discreetly in the overflow menu, sending flagged cases to admin moderation.
The Saved tab is visual: a grid of listing cards, each small but still framed with gold. Long pressing on a saved card shakes it lightly and reveals a red “Remove” option. A share icon in the top corner lets the user forward listings to contacts.
Profile is minimal but personal. At the top is a circular avatar framed with a glowing gold ring, with an edit pencil icon floating in the corner. Beneath are three sections: Applications, Saved Searches, and Account Settings. Account Settings themselves open into standard options like password change, notification toggles, and account deletion. Everything is black and gold, consistent, unchanging.
For providers, the experience begins at a dashboard. A gold header greets them by name. Below, three large tiles fill the top of the page: one displaying a large number of “Beds Available” in bold gold on black, the next showing “New Applications” with a badge count, and the third offering a glowing gold “Add New Listing” button. Beneath lies a list of “My Listings,” each card showing a thumbnail photo, address, available beds, last updated time, and two buttons: Edit and View.
Adding a new listing is a structured wizard. At the very top is a horizontal row of gold dots marking eleven steps, each step a full screen of fields grouped by theme. Basic Info comes first, asking for property name and address with Google Places autocomplete, then confirming the housing type from a grid of gold icons. Units and beds come next, then Amenities, then Accessibility, then Eligibility, and so on. Each form is built the same way: black background, white outlined inputs that glow gold when active, toggles and sliders filling gold when chosen. At the photo step, thumbnails appear in a grid, draggable for reordering, with one highlighted as cover. The preview step shows the listing as seekers will see it, with a checkbox “I accept terms.” The final step is a bold gold “Publish” button. Once published, a prompt appears encouraging them to set a daily bed availability reminder, offering quick calendar add-ins for Google, Microsoft, or Apple, defaulting to 7 pm local time.
Updating availability is fast. Providers see a table of their listings, each row with title, last updated time, and plus/minus controls for bed counts. At the bottom, a gold “Save All” bar stretches across. If a listing hasn’t been updated in 48 hours, a gold banner appears at the top of the screen reading “Data may be stale. Confirm no changes or update counts,” with a “Confirm no change today” button.
Applications for providers are managed in a pipeline view, styled like a kanban board. The columns stretch across the screen from left to right: New Inquiry, Missing Documents, Under Review, Approved, Move-In Pending, Active Resident, Completed, Rejected, Waitlist. Each column is outlined in gold, each card black with the seeker’s name in white, small gold tags showing eligibility markers, and a timestamp for how long they’ve been in the stage. Providers can drag and drop applications between stages, though moving into a decision stage like Approved or Rejected requires a small gold modal note explaining the decision.
Clicking on a card opens the applicant profile. The header shows their name in bold gold, contact details, application ID, and the listing they applied to. Below are tabs: Overview, Documents, Notes, Messages, History. The Overview shows a score match and summary. Documents presents a checklist with status indicators: missing, uploaded, verified, rejected. Providers can request new documents, mark them verified, or reject them with reasons. Notes are private only to the provider. Messages link to the conversation thread. History lists every action with a timestamp in white on black. Once all required documents are verified, a gold “Export Packet” button appears, bundling everything into a single PDF.
Hospitals, medical respite centers, and detox facilities exist seamlessly in search results alongside housing, differentiated by their gold badges — “5150 capable,” “Medical respite,” or “Detox.” Their listing cards include direct intake phone lines, intake hours, and triage notes. If they participate in daily updates, they display “Beds today” and “Beds this week” like standard housing. Opening their detail view feels the same as housing listings, but with additional fields for eligibility and referral requirements, always accompanied by a disclaimer in white reminding seekers to dial emergency numbers if in crisis.
Throughout the app, the black-and-gold design language never wavers. Buttons are always large, bold, and high contrast. Inputs are always outlined, glowing gold on focus. Empty states are reassuring, like “No matches yet. Try widening your filters” in white on black with a single gold action button below. Errors are clear, always in red, never subtle. Accessibility is paramount: every gold-on-black heading is tested for contrast, every icon-only button carries labels for screen readers, every focus ring glows gold.
The app feels like Airbnb reborn for a different mission: searching, applying, managing, and updating housing with dignity, speed, and clarity. The experience is intentionally minimal, elegant, and bold, stripped down to what matters: seekers find stability, providers keep their supply accurate, and both sides communicate easily. No wasted clicks, no hidden steps, no visual noise. Just black, gold, and the sense that safety and second chances are finally within reach.
